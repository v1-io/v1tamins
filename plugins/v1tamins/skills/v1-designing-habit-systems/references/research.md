## 1. Implementation intentions

**Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006)**, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 38, 69–119. Meta-analysis of **94 independent tests, d = .65** (medium-to-large effect on goal attainment).

- Pre-deciding *when/where/how* ("if [cue], then I [action]") delegates initiation to the environmental cue; action stops depending on in-the-moment motivation or memory.
- Effective for initiating action, shielding ongoing pursuit from distraction, and disengaging from failing approaches.
- Vague intentions ("exercise more") underperform the if-then format by a wide margin.

**Design implication:** never leave a behavior as a floating intention; always bind it to a concrete cue.

## 2. Habits live in context

**Wood & Neal (2007)**, *Psychological Review* 114(4), 843; **Wood & Rünger (2016)**, *Annual Review of Psychology* 67; **Neal, Wood, Labrecque & Lally (2012)**, *JESP* 48.

- Experience-sampling: **~43% of daily actions are performed almost daily in the same context**.
- Strong habits are triggered by context (location, preceding action) and are *insensitive to current goals* — bad habits survive good intentions; good habits survive low-motivation days.
- Habit strength grows from repetition in a *stable* context; context instability is the main formation killer.

**Design implication:** anchor quality = context stability (daily, externally enforced, same place, same preceding event). Redesign the environment rather than the person.

## 3. The formation runway

**Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010)**, *European Journal of Social Psychology* 40(6), 998–1009.

- 96 participants, one new daily behavior in a fixed context. **Median 66 days to the automaticity plateau; range 18–254.**
- Automaticity follows an asymptotic curve — early repetitions contribute most.
- **Missing a single day did not materially derail formation.** Consecutive misses and context changes do the damage.

**Design implication:** set a 2–3 month expectation; judge weeks not days; one miss is noise, two consecutive misses is a design defect.

## 4. Temptation bundling

**Milkman, Minson & Volpp (2014)**, "Holding the Hunger Games Hostage at the Gym", *Management Science* 60(2), 283–299; replication/extension **Kirgios et al. (2020)**, *OBHDP* 161.

- Gym-only access to page-turner audiobooks: **+51% gym visits** (device-enforced), +29% (self-managed) vs control.
- Effects decay after schedule disruptions (the original study's post-Thanksgiving drop); bundles need deliberate re-establishment after breaks.
- 61% of participants were later willing to pay for the restriction — commitment demand is real.

**Design implication:** reserve a specific pleasure exclusively for the avoided behavior; rebuild the bundle explicitly after any break.

## 5. Fresh starts and habit discontinuity

**Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014)**, "The Fresh Start Effect", *Management Science* 60(10), 2563–2582; **Verplanken & Roy (2016)** field experiment and UK Understanding Society evidence (habit discontinuity hypothesis).

- Temporal landmarks open new "mental accounting periods": gym attendance rises **~33% at the start of a new week** alone; larger jumps at new year, birthdays, post-holiday.
- Habit discontinuity: interventions are markedly more effective within ~3 months of a context change (moving house, new job, new child) because old cue-response links are suspended.
- Corollary: the same disruptions dismantle good habits — returning from travel is when systems collapse.

**Design implication:** launch on a landmark; treat major life transitions as the highest-leverage design windows; write a re-entry plan before every planned disruption.

## 6. Commitment devices

**Milkman et al. (2014)** temptation-bundling study: 61% of participants later willing to pay for device-enforced restriction — evidence that people value pre-commitment when stakes are real.

- Booked/prepaid slots, filled carts, and social commitments work because cancelling must cost more than ignoring.
- A bare calendar block has no stakes; it is a floating intention (pillar 1), not a commitment device.

**Design implication:** add a commitment device wherever stakes matter; never treat a calendar block alone as sufficient.

## 7. Friction asymmetry

Follows directly from pillar 2: context triggers beat intentions.

- Remove steps from the desired path (gear pre-staged, order pre-placed, app on home screen).
- Add steps to the undesired path (app off home screen, snack out of reach, card left at home).

**Design implication:** redesign the environment so the good path is the path of least resistance.

## 8. Objective review over nudges

**Lally et al. (2010)** and self-monitoring literature: sustained behavior change depends on feedback loops, not one-time motivation.

- Reminder notifications habituate quickly; pings are ignored after days or weeks.
- A periodic review of measured data (wearable, transactions, receipts) sustains self-monitoring where nudges fail.

**Design implication:** schedule a weekly review against objective adherence data; do not rely on push reminders as the primary sustain mechanism.
