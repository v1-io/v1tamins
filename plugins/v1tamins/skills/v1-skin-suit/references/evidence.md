# Evidence and limits

Research reviewed September 2026. These findings motivate contextual choices;
they do not validate this skill or a universal recipe for human writing.

| Evidence | Useful implication | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Kemp et al., 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0736585325000036): 200 Australian undergraduates rated final periods in simulated texts; shorter replies were read more negatively. | A short casual reply may omit its final period. | Emotion ratings, not naturalness; the effect did not hold for six-to-eight-word replies. |
| [Fang et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39541519/): eight studies, 5,306 participants, found abbreviations reduced sincerity and willingness to reply. | Avoid manufacturing shorthand as a human signal. | Abbreviations are not ordinary contractions; context matters. |
| [Abirou et al., 2024](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/edbcaff5-1ee9-41a0-bd39-d02628603290/content): lowercase carried social and stylistic associations. | Casing belongs to reader, channel, and author preference. | Lowercase is not universally warmer or more effective. |
| [Sourati et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11266): rewriting reduced variation in writing complexity across large datasets. | Preserve good wording and individual expression; edit minimally. | Population-level variation is not a measure of one author's likeness. |
| [Wang et al., EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.532/): informal author styles were harder to imitate than structured genres. | Relevant examples help form a hypothesis, not a guarantee. | Computational authorship measures do not establish reader preference for short messages. |
| [PersonaMail, IUI 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17340): a 16-person study combined relationship context and reusable editing preferences. | Learn narrow preferences from actual corrections. | Small sample and bundled interventions do not isolate a prompting effect. |
| [Gao et al., ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1998/): personalization can reverse features used by text detectors. | Evaluate meaning and reader judgment, not detector scores. | Detector robustness is a different outcome from writing quality. |

Additional approaches worth testing include explicit style hypotheses
([HyPerAlign](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00038)), iterative preference refinement
([PROSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23815)), and author features with contrastive
examples ([Yazan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08745)). None justifies a new
retrieval system or automatic preference learning for a small writing task.

[Wispr's personalized-style post](https://wisprflow.ai/post/personalized-style)
usefully separates typography from wording. It is product guidance, not
independent evidence of writing quality. Public humanizer skills can offer
useful source comparisons, but numerical rhythm quotas, forced errors, and
mandatory stylistic patterns merely replace one formula with another.
